FAQ

Common questions

Everything you need to know about A/B testing statistics and how this calculator works.

01 What p-value is statistically significant?

At 95% confidence, a p-value below 0.05 is significant. At 99% confidence, you need p < 0.01. A smaller p-value means stronger evidence that the difference is real. This calculator adjusts thresholds for your chosen confidence level and applies Bonferroni correction automatically for multi-variant tests.

02 How many visitors do I need?

Sample size depends on four factors: baseline conversion rate, minimum effect size (MDE), desired statistical power (typically 80%), and significance level (typically 95%). Use the Power Analysis tab to see exact sample sizes for various MDE values.

sample-size mde power
03 What is statistical power in A/B testing?

Power is the probability of detecting a real effect when one exists. Aim for ≥80% power. Low power means your test might be inconclusive even if the variant truly performs differently. The calculator shows current power and the sample size needed for 80% power.

04 When should I use Bonferroni correction?

Use Bonferroni correction when testing multiple variants simultaneously (A/B/C or A/B/C/D). Without correction, testing 3 variants at 95% confidence gives a 14% chance of at least one false positive. Applied automatically here for 3+ variants.

05 What is a Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM)?

SRM occurs when the observed traffic split differs significantly from the intended split. It often indicates a bug in randomization or bot traffic. SRM can invalidate results even if the p-value looks significant. Detected using a chi-square test with a 1% threshold.

srm chi-square randomization
06 One-sided or two-sided test?

Two-sided tests are recommended for most A/B tests because they protect against both positive and negative outcomes. A one-sided test only checks if the variant is better, making it easier to reach significance but ignoring potential harm.

How to Use This Calculator

1. Enter Your Data

Input visitors and conversions for control and at least one variant. You can rename each variant (e.g. "amazon_A_Group") by clicking the name field.

2. Configure Settings

Set confidence level (default 95%), hypothesis type (two-sided recommended), and variant count.

3. Read Your Results

Results tab shows verdict, lift, p-value, power, Wilson confidence intervals, distribution chart, and SRM check. Bonferroni correction is automatic for multi-variant tests.

4. Go Deeper

Power Analysis: Required sample sizes and power curve.
Monte Carlo Simulation: 5,000 randomized trials.
Insights: Plain-English recommendations with projected business impact.

5. Export

Download a multi-sheet Excel report with Summary, Insights, Simulation, and Power Analysis.

Methodology

Test: Two-proportion Z-test with signed z-statistic.

CIs: Wilson interval (more accurate for small samples and extreme rates).

Power: Single-term non-central normal approximation.

Multiple comparisons: Bonferroni correction for 3+ variants.

SRM: Chi-square test (Wilson-Hilferty approximation).

Simulation: True binomial Monte Carlo, 5,000 trials.
