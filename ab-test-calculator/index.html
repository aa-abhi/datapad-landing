Frequently Asked Questions

What p-value is statistically significant for an A/B test? ›
At 95% confidence (the standard), a p-value below 0.05 is significant. At 99% confidence, you need p < 0.01. A smaller p-value means stronger evidence that the difference is real, not due to chance. This calculator shows the threshold adjusted for your chosen confidence level and applies Bonferroni correction for multi-variant tests.
How many visitors do I need for an A/B test? ›
Sample size depends on four factors: baseline conversion rate, the minimum effect size you want to detect (MDE), desired statistical power (typically 80%), and significance level (typically 95%). Use the Power Analysis tab to see exact sample sizes for various MDE values given your baseline rate and current traffic.
What is statistical power in A/B testing? ›
Power is the probability of detecting a real effect when one exists (avoiding false negatives). Aim for ≥80% power. Low power means your test might be inconclusive even if the variant truly performs differently. The calculator shows current power and the sample size needed to achieve 80% power for your observed effect size.
When should I use Bonferroni correction? ›
Use Bonferroni correction when testing multiple variants simultaneously (A/B/C or A/B/C/D). Without correction, testing 3 variants at 95% confidence gives a 14% chance of at least one false positive. Bonferroni divides alpha by the number of comparisons, automatically applied here for 3+ variant tests.
What is a Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM)? ›
SRM occurs when the observed traffic split differs significantly from the intended split. It often indicates a bug in your randomization, a redirect issue, or bot traffic. SRM can invalidate your results even if the p-value looks significant. This calculator detects SRM using a chi-square test with a 1% threshold.

How to Use This Calculator

1. Enter Your Data

Input visitors and conversions for control and at least one variant. For revenue tests, enable Revenue mode and enter total revenue per variant.

2. Configure Settings

Set confidence level (default 95%), hypothesis type (two-sided recommended), and variant count. Hover the i icons to learn what each metric means.

3. Read Your Results

The Results tab shows verdict, lift, p-value, power, Wilson confidence intervals, distribution chart, and SRM check. Bonferroni correction is automatic for multi-variant tests.

4. Go Deeper

Power Analysis: Required sample sizes and a visual power curve.
Monte Carlo Simulation: 5,000 randomized trials for probabilistic outcomes.
Insights: Plain-English recommendations with projected business impact.

5. Export

Download a multi-sheet Excel report with Summary, Insights, Simulation, and Power Analysis.

Statistical Methodology

Test: Two-proportion Z-test with signed z-statistic for correct one-sided direction.

CIs: Wilson interval (more accurate than Wald for small samples and extreme rates).

Power: Single-term non-central normal approximation.

Multiple comparisons: Bonferroni correction for 3+ variants.

SRM: Chi-square test for sample ratio mismatch detection.

Simulation: True binomial Monte Carlo, 5,000 trials (normal approximation for n > 1000).
